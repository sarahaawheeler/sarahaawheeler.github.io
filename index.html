AI can be harnessed for the wrong reasons. How can we mitigate its risk?

Policy Comment

I contributed to a policy comment on the misuse risks of dual-use foundation models. What makes these systems interesting, and difficult to govern, is that the capabilities that make them useful for everyday tasks can also make them dangerous. Working on this gave me a chance to think about AI beyond its technical capabilities, particularly the questions of responsibility and governance that follow from them.

About Me

I first became interested in data science through election forecasting (insert mandatory shout-out to Steve Kornacki). I was taken by the idea that statistics could reveal to us something meaningful about human behavior, despite how unpredictable it can be. My interests have since expanded well beyond election forecasting, though I think much of my curiosity still comes from the same place. I want to understand how technology influences people's decisions, what happens when we begin trusting machines to make them for us, and how we can build systems that are reliable and auditable. I've had the opportunity to try to answer some of these questions through my agentic work at 84.51° and my research at Illinois on human–AI interaction.

While I enjoy the technical side of data science, I am just as interested in the questions that can't necessarily be answered by a model. Who benefits (and who does not) from the technologies we develop? What assumptions are we making about the people who will use them? How and where should we keep a human in the loop?

If any of these questions interest you as much as they do me, I'd love to chat ↗ .

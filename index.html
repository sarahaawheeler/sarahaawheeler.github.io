About

I first became interested in data science through election forecasting (insert mandatory shout-out to Steve Kornacki). I was taken by the idea that statistics could reveal to us something meaningful about human behavior, despite how unpredictable it can be. At the time, I was also studying political science, and after a few courses and a summer working in a Congressional office, I realized that what interested me most about elections was not necessarily politics, but the people behind them.

My interests have since expanded well beyond election forecasting, though I think much of my curiosity still comes from the same place. I want to understand how technology influences people's decisions, what happens when we begin trusting machines to make them for us, and how we can build systems that are reliable and auditable. I've had the opportunity to try to answer some of these questions through my agentic work at 84.51° and my research at Illinois on human–AI interaction.

I enjoy the technical side of data science, but am just as interested in the questions that can't necessarily be answered by a model. Who benefits (and who does not) from the technologies we develop? What assumptions are we making about the people who will use them? How and where should we keep a human in the loop?

If any of these questions interest you as much as they do me, I'd love to chat with you ↗ .

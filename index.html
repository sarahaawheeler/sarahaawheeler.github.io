Research and applied projects

What should we delegate to AI?

Research

When an AI system can take on a task, does that mean we want it to? I lead a survey study of 366 participants examining how people think about delegating everyday activities and decisions to AI, including questions of trust, autonomy, and oversight.

Human–AI interaction · iTEA Lab · Manuscript under review

Can a conversation help someone change a habit?

Research

In the ACTION Lab, I've studied a motivational interviewing chatbot designed to support healthier choices. I'm interested not just in whether conversational AI can help, but how people respond to it—and what makes that support useful in practice.

Health informatics · Human behavior · ACTION Lab

What can data tell us about financial resilience?

Applied ML

Through Break Through Tech, I'm working with Federal Reserve survey data to model financial vulnerability. Beyond predictive performance, the project considers calibration and interpretability: what can we understand from a model, and what should we be careful not to conclude?

Machine learning · Interpretability · Work in progress

About

I first became interested in data science through election forecasting (insert mandatory shout-out to Steve Kornacki). I was taken by the idea that statistics could tell us something meaningful about human behavior, despite how unpredictable it can be. At the time, I was also studying political science, and after a few courses and a summer working in a Congressional office, I realized that what interested me most about elections was not necessarily politics, but the people behind them.

My interests have since expanded well beyond election forecasting, though I think much of my curiosity still comes from the same place. I want to understand how people make decisions, how technology influences those decisions, and what happens when we begin trusting machines to make them for us. I've had the opportunity to try to answer some of these questions through my work at 84.51° and my research at Illinois on human–AI interaction.

I enjoy the technical side of data science, but I'm just as interested in the questions that can't necessarily be answered with a model. Who benefits from the technologies we develop? What assumptions are we making about the people who will use them? And how do we decide what problems are worth solving in the first place?
